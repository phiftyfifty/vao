That's the usual spot for kids who grew up hearing Lao at home. Vao starts from listening and gets you to speaking, without a classroom.

01

Hear it first

Every lesson opens with sound. You pick the meaning by ear before you ever see a spelling, the same way you learned it at home.

02

Build it in Lao script

Put phrases together from real Lao words, not just transliteration. Romanization is there when you need it and turns off when you don't.

03

Say it, then compare

Record yourself and play it back next to a native speaker. Anything you miss comes back on a spaced schedule until it sticks.

The best Lao audio isn't in an app. It's your mom saying it.

Vao lets a family member record the words in your lessons, so the voice you learn from is the one you'll hear at the dinner table, accent and all.

Everyday words come first: family, food, getting around, the things people actually say at home and at the temple.

Questions

Who is it for?

Mostly people with Lao family who want to speak with them: second-generation kids, partners, in-laws. It also works if you're moving to Laos or heading there for work.

Do I need to read Lao already?

No. Romanization is on by default and every word has audio. You can switch it off once the script starts to feel familiar.

Is it free?

Yes while it's in beta. The core lessons are meant to stay free.

iPhone or Android?

It runs in your phone's browser and works offline once it's loaded, so both.
